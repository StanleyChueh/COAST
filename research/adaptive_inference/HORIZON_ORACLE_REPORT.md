## 4. Noise calibration (rule frozen before execution)

Each arm is a single stochastic rollout per scene. Flow noise comes from a running RNG, and the closed loop amplifies small differences. A per-scene "any arm succeeds" oracle therefore rewards having **more draws**, whether or not the horizon matters. The replicate arms measure this directly. The calibration set is 200 identical scenes (CoffeeSetupMug, OpenDrawer, PickPlaceCounterToCabinet, PickPlaceCounterToStove × seeds 0–49), every one hash-matched.

| pair on the same 200 scenes | rates | discordance | McNemar p | 2-arm oracle | oracle gain over better arm [95%] |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| **same h**: fixed16 (B) vs fixed16 (A-U) | 47.5 / 50.0 | **25.5%** | 0.58 | 61.5% | **+11.5 pp** [7.5, 14.5] |
| **same h**: fixed8 (B) vs fixed8 (A-U) | 51.0 / 48.5 | 24.5% | 0.57 | 62.0% | +11.0 pp [7.0, 14.0] |
| **same method**: AAC (B) vs AAC (A-U) | 50.5 / 50.0 | 22.5% | 1.00 | 61.5% | +11.0 pp [6.5, 13.0] |
| different h: fixed16 (B) vs fixed8 (B) | 47.5 / 51.0 | 23.5% | 0.38 | 61.0% | +10.0 pp [6.0, 13.5] |
| different h: fixed16 (B) vs fixed8 (A-U) | 47.5 / 48.5 | 19.0% | 0.87 | 57.5% | +9.0 pp [5.0, 11.5] |
| different h: fixed16 (A-U) vs fixed8 (B) | 50.0 / 51.0 | 24.0% | 0.89 | 62.5% | +11.5 pp [7.0, 14.0] |
| fixed16 (B) vs AAC (B) | 47.5 / 50.5 | 23.0% | 0.46 | 60.5% | +10.0 pp [6.0, 13.0] |

The frozen decision statistic is the paired difference in 2-arm oracle gain, (different h) − (same h), with a task-stratified bootstrap:

| contrast | Δ gain | 95% CI |
|---|---:|---|
| {16B, 8B} − {16B, 16U} | **−1.5 pp** | [−5.5, +3.0] |
| {16B, 8U} − {16B, 16U} | −2.5 pp | [−6.5, +1.5] |
| {16U, 8B} − {16B, 16U} | 0.0 pp | [−5.0, +4.0] |
| {16B, AAC B} − {16B, 16U} | −1.5 pp | [−5.5, +3.0] |

**The criterion fails.** Re-running the *same* horizon recovers as many "rescues" as switching horizon (point estimates ≤ 0). Two runs of the same arm on the same scene disagree on 22–26% of scenes. This is the same rate as two different horizons (19–24%).

A sensitivity check gives the same answer. A null with no scene × horizon interaction, where each scene has one latent success probability with a Beta distribution matched to the observed mean and to the replicate flip rate (0.25), predicts:
- a 5-draw oracle of **73.5%**, against an observed 71.4%;
- a 2-draw oracle of 60.3%, against an observed 60–62.5%.

The whole 5-way horizon "headroom" is the size expected from five independent draws of one policy.

## 5. Verdict for the temporal intervention

- **Raw gate (as specified): HIGH** (+20.3 pp).
- **Noise-calibrated (frozen validity rule): not distinguishable from rollout noise.** On this stack, the per-scene horizon oracle measures how stochastic the policy is, not horizon complementarity.
  - The only reproducible horizon effect is the **marginal** one: h = 2 is worse, and fixed16 is best on average.
  - A cross-fitted per-task horizon choice (47.0%) does **worse** than always using fixed16 (51.0%).
- Therefore the **temporal intervention is LOW PRIORITY** as a routing target. Any state-level horizon choice would compete for at most the marginal differences between h ∈ {4, 8, 12, 16} (≤ 3.3 pp on matched scenes), and no state-level structure is detectable beyond noise.

## 6. Consequences for the rest of the programme

1. **Rollout noise dominates single-draw oracles on GR00T N1.5 + RoboCasa.**
   - Outcome flip probability between two replicate rollouts of the same scene: about 0.25.
   - A Stage-5 "same-state, one draw per intervention" oracle would show large fake complementarity, by the same mechanism.
   - Any same-state intervention matrix therefore needs (a) a **base-vs-base replicate branch as a negative control** and (b) several noise draws per (state, intervention), or exact common random numbers. Even with those, closed-loop divergence re-randomises the outcome after the first differing action. This requirement is carried into `PILOT_PREREG.md` as a validity condition.
2. **"Resampling the base policy" is itself a strong intervention family.** Best-of-2 of the *same* arm gains about 11 pp on these scenes. Any routing claim has to beat simple re-sampling and retry at matched compute. Reuse Before You Retrieve (arXiv 2608.17484) reports recoverable headroom of this kind as its main quantity.
3. Phase-B AAC data contain **no evidence** that execution horizon is a state-dependent treatment on this stack. A horizon branch in a later portfolio has to be justified by same-state evidence. It cannot rest on these aggregate data.
